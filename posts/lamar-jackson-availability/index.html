Lamar Jackson and the Problem With Counting Starts

Allen and Mahomes have been hurt, too. They have also been available far more often. For Baltimore, the missing Sundays matter.

Salamander Jefferson · Updated October 20, 2026 · Regular-season games through October 19
Jump to:Allen and MahomesPlaying hurtBaltimore’s resultsThe playoff stakesThe counterargumentsHistorical rankingsLamar’s absencesThe division swing

Lamar Jackson missed his second straight game with an ankle injury on Sunday. The Ravens scored ten points, lost to Cleveland and surrendered their outright division lead. That absence prompted me to write this article, because it feels like Baltimore constantly has to weather the absence of its best player, and some Jackson defenders appear unwilling to acknowledge that this is a problem.

One of the stranger defenses is the graphic pointing out that Lamar has the fifth-most quarterback starts since 2018. Only seven quarterbacks have remained established starters since taking over in 2018 or earlier (Jackson, Allen, Mahomes, Goff, Prescott, Stafford and Rodgers). Most of the guys below him weren’t starters for that entire stretch. Some hadn’t even entered the league. If we’re measuring durability, the useful question is how many games a quarterback played out of the games he was expected to start. We’re not impressed that Lamar has more raw starts since 2018 than Justin Herbert when Herbert started his career two years later.

Of those seven, Allen and Mahomes are the two Lamar is most often compared to. That comparison isn’t particularly flattering here. Since 2018, Lamar has missed 16 expected starts, compared with four for Allen and five for Mahomes. He has also had six separate absence episodes, compared with one for Allen and two for Mahomes. We spend an awful lot of time splitting hairs between these quarterbacks. How often their teams actually get to play with them seems like a pretty important distinction.

Allen’s four missed starts all came during one stretch of his rookie season in 2018. One injury, four games. He has not missed another expected start for health reasons since.

Mahomes missed two games in 2019, then the final three games of last season after tearing his ACL. His entire missed-start total comes from two injuries, one of which required reconstructive knee surgery.

Lamar has now missed multiple games in four of the last six seasons. That is the frustrating part. Allen’s absences are concentrated in one stretch eight years ago. Mahomes’ include a torn ACL. With Lamar, the Ravens keep having to revisit the same question: how long are we going to be without our best player this time?

They have been hurt, too

And Allen’s availability hasn’t come from avoiding the injury report. He played through a throwing-elbow injury in 2022 that forced him to adjust his mechanics, a broken non-throwing hand in 2024 and a broken bone in his right foot during last season’s playoff run. He had surgery on that foot in January. These were real injuries. Buffalo still got to put its best player on the field.

Mahomes won the Super Bowl for the 2022 season on a high ankle sprain. He injured it against Jacksonville, threw for 326 yards and two touchdowns against Cincinnati eight days later, then aggravated it during the Super Bowl and still led Kansas City to the title. Staying on the field and finding a way to remain effective while hurt is part of what makes these guys great. It belongs in the comparison just as much as what they do when everything is working.

That is what makes Lamar’s recurring absences so despicable. His missed games haven’t come from one devastating injury that wiped out an entire season. They have come in separate stretches, repeatedly forcing Baltimore to turn its offense over to a backup. When the quarterbacks he is competing with for MVPs and a place atop the AFC have shown the ability to play through injuries, expecting more reliable availability from Lamar is hardly an unreasonable standard.

The Ravens have needed him

And Baltimore feels those absences. Since Lamar took over as the starter, the Ravens are 79–32 in his regular-season starts. In the 16 injury or illness absences counted here, they are 6–10. That is a winning percentage of 71.2% with him and 37.5% without him. For a perennial contender, these absences have been costly.

These were not all disposable October games

Take 2021. The Ravens were 8–3 before losing their final six games. Lamar injured his ankle during the second loss of that stretch and missed the final four games. Baltimore lost all four, three by a combined five points, and finished 8–9 without a playoff berth. Those are the margins a franchise quarterback is paid to change. Instead, Baltimore had to navigate the stretch run without him, and it cost them a chance to compete for the Super Bowl.

In 2022, the problem followed Baltimore into the playoffs. Lamar missed the final five regular-season games with a knee injury, and the Ravens went 2–3. They still made the postseason, then had to play their wild-card game without him and lost 24–17 to Cincinnati. Another season ended with Baltimore’s best player unavailable when it mattered most.

In 2025, the backups actually kept Baltimore afloat, going 2–2 across two separate injury absences: three games with a hamstring injury and one with a back injury. The bigger issue was what the Ravens got after his first return. He threw four touchdowns against Miami in Week 9, but across his nine remaining starts, his completion percentage fell from 71.6% to 59.9%, his passer rating from 130.5 to 91.5, and his QB EPA per dropback from +0.249 to +0.016. Despite returning to the field, he clearly wasn’t able to perform near his normal level of excellence. Baltimore went 5–4 in those starts, but lost four of his final five and missed the playoffs at 8–9. His diminished production hurt a team that needed its franchise quarterback to carry it through the stretch run.

The pro-Lamar arguments deserve answers

And before somebody points to rest games or COVID, those adjustments are already baked into the numbers. Lamar’s deliberate rest in the 2019 and 2023 finales does not count against him. Neither does his 2020 COVID absence. We gave everybody the same grace for illness that season. Lamar has also played hurt, and he had no injury or illness absences in 2023 or 2024. Those seasons deserve credit. The 16 missed starts that remain are the problem we’re discussing.

The historical context still matters

To put that in historical context, we went back to 2000 and looked at 41 quarterbacks with at least 100 regular-season starts during that span. Lamar ranks 27th in availability and has the sixth-highest rate of separate injury or illness absences per season as the starter. He falls below the middle of the group for games available and near the top for how often a new health problem interrupts his season. Bottom line: Lamar has missed games at a higher frequency than his peers.

At the top of the list, Eli Manning and Philip Rivers had no injury or illness absences that cost them an expected start. Matt Ryan missed three in 237 opportunities. Drew Brees missed ten in 296. Tom Brady missed 15, all from the same knee injury in 2008. These guys carried their franchises for years, and their teams could count on having them on the field. That kind of reliability deserves more appreciation.

Now narrow the field to quarterbacks who have won an NFL MVP or started a Super Bowl victory. Seventeen meet those criteria, including Lamar. He ranks 14th in availability and has the third-highest rate of separate injury or illness absences per season as the starter. If we’re putting Lamar at the big boys’ table, it’s safe to say he’s one of the more injury-prone among them.

The two absences in 2025 were separate problems. Lamar missed three games with the hamstring injury, returned to start eight straight games, then missed Week 17 with a back injury. This year’s ankle injury adds another interruption in another season. That is the pattern that stands out: different health problems repeatedly taking him out of the lineup, including twice in the same year.

And now the division lead is gone

That brings us back to Cleveland. Huntley led Baltimore to a win in Atlanta in Lamar’s first ankle absence. In the second, the Ravens scored ten points and lost 13–10 to the Browns. I don’t think it’s unreasonable to assume Baltimore wins that game with Lamar. Instead of sitting at 5–1 with a key division win in their back pocket, they’re now third in the division based on tiebreakers. That type of loss will matter for seeding come January.

Availability belongs in the comparison

Lamar’s best football belongs alongside anybody’s. That is why his availability deserves this level of scrutiny. Baltimore has repeatedly had to fight for its season without the player its offense is built around, while Allen and Mahomes have been much more reliable for their teams. That is a meaningful advantage. It should count when we compare them, rather than getting brushed aside by fanboys who can’t handle even minor criticism of their favorite player.

Sources, definitions and data notes

We counted regular-season starts and health-related nonstarts during each quarterback’s starting role. Rookie backup weeks, healthy benchings and deliberate rest are excluded. Injuries and illness count, with one exception: illness in 2020. Those COVID-year absences remain in the raw data.

Expected starts = starts made + counted health nonstarts
Missed-game percentage = health nonstarts ÷ expected starts

Expected starts and role changes

Expected starts are actual QB starts plus medically missed starts during an assigned starting role. An incumbent’s injury keeps opportunities open until recovery, a documented permanent role change, or the end of his tenure. Entire injured incumbent seasons count. Healthy benchings, retirement, time out of the league, deliberate whole-game rest, byes and canceled games do not. Injury replacements can accrue starting opportunities while the incumbent also has medical nonstarts; player opportunities need not sum to one per team-game.

Suspensions are not injuries. Brady’s four and Roethlisberger’s four suspension nonstarts, plus Newton’s disciplinary nonstart, are separate in the data. An all-cause sensitivity is also supplied. “Raw” in the table below means health absences before the 2020 illness exception.

Episodes and starter seasons

One continuous health-related absence is one episode. A return to the starting lineup followed by a new missed-start spell is a separate interruption, including recurrences. A separately documented health problem can also split consecutive nonstarts. A continuing episode can span seasons; Wentz’s 2017 ACL recovery into 2018 is one. Conditions that never caused a missed expected start do not enter the episode count.

Starter-season counts include partial and ongoing years, multi-game interim roles, and an entire season lost by an incumbent to injury. Isolated backup spots are excluded: Mahomes’ 2017 start counts in availability but does not add a starter season. Vick’s two isolated 2001 replacement starts are treated the same way. The denominator is a season count, not a count of 16- or 17-game equivalents.

2020 illness exception

Only illness in the 2020 season is excused. Injuries in that season still count; illness and COVID in other seasons count. Lamar, Newton and Dalton each have one excused 2020 nonstart in this field. Dalton’s preceding concussion absence remains counted. Those records remain in the export and the retained 2020 audit.

Ranks and limitations

Rank 1 in availability is best. Interruption frequency is discussed from highest to lowest. Ranks use unrounded rates, with ties sharing rank. Lamar’s comparison percentile is the share of the other 40 quarterbacks with lower availability: 14 ÷ 40 = 35.0%. The median is the median player rate, not a pooled league rate.

Scenario checks count Rodgers’ last two 2017 nonstarts as medical, add Ryan’s two 2022 shoulder/benching nonstarts, and excuse Vick’s 2004 mixed shoulder/rest game, separately and together. They preserve Lamar’s 27th-place availability and sixth-highest frequency ranks. These scenarios are not exhaustive uncertainty bounds. The classification notes retain the decision points; historical interpretation should not outrun the sources.

Starting 100 games is itself a selection filter. This study does not estimate leaguewide injury incidence, prove an era effect, measure full-game participation, or diagnose future risk. Both of Lamar’s completed 2026 ankle absences are included. Future games, including announced absences, are not counted.
